# CO₂ removals and negative-emitting countries
**When:** 18/11, time n/a · **Where:** Group of Negative Emitters (GONE) event, Antalya · **Organiser:** GONE (co-chairs Denmark and Ethiopia)<br>
**Speakers:** **Inger Andersen** (UNEP Executive Director); **Samuel Addis Alemayehu** (Ethiopian Ministry of Foreign Affairs); representatives of Denmark and Panama<br>
🎥 Video not yet available

## At a glance
The Group of Negative Emitters (GONE) brings together states aiming to absorb more CO₂ than they emit. The topic matters because the first draft of the COP31 deal contains a general goal to scale up CO₂ removal, which divides countries.

## Key points
- **Inger Andersen, Executive Director of UNEP (UN Environment Programme)**, said that even with deep cuts the world is heading for 1.7–1.8°C by mid-century ⚠️ according to Climate Home [1].
- **Inger Andersen** estimated that 320 billion tonnes of CO₂ must be removed this century ⚠️ [1]. Nature currently removes about 2 billion tonnes a year ⚠️ [1].
- **Denmark** announced EUR 40 million for Ethiopia's forest programme ⚠️ [1]. **Climate Home** notes that some "net-negative" claims rest on questionable forest carbon accounting ⚠️ [1].
- **The first draft of the final decision** contains a goal to scale up CO₂ removal; **the European Union** opposes the wording ⚠️ according to Climate Home [2].
- **Ilana Seid, Palau's ambassador**, warned against marine removals (ocean geoengineering): "a lot of the science there is unproven" ⚠️ [2].

## Quotes
- "Net zero is no longer a destination, it's a waypoint." — **Inger Andersen, UNEP Executive Director** ⚠️ [1]

## Relevance for the VSME
**Medium** — No obligation. Removals and carbon credits must be reported separately: an SME should not net them against the gross emissions reported in B3.
