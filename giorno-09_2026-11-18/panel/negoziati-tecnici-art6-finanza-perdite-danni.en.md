# Technical negotiations: Art. 6.4, finance, loss and damage
**When:** 18/11, all day · **Where:** negotiating rooms, Antalya EXPO Center · **Organiser:** COP31 Presidency of Negotiations and UNFCCC co-facilitators<br>
**Speakers:** **Sally Box** (COP31 Presidency of Negotiations); **Ayşin Turpancı** (COP31 Presidency); **Malin Ahlberg** (Germany) and **Sonam Tashi** (Bhutan), co-facilitators on Art. 6.4; **Jose Delgado** (Austria), co-facilitator on the loss and damage Fund<br>
🎥 No video expected (informal consultations)

## At a glance
Many political discussions have moved to ministerial pairs, but the technical rooms worked on revised texts. The only agreement of the day concerns the least developed countries.

## Key points
- **Co-facilitators Malin Ahlberg and Sonam Tashi** presented a revised text on guidance to the Supervisory Body of the Article 6.4 mechanism (the UN carbon crediting market) ⚠️ according to ENB [1].
- **AOSIS (the alliance of small island states)** wants to clarify that units forwarded to the Adaptation Fund can be monetised and that their authorisation cannot change afterwards ⚠️ [1]. **The Arab Group** prefers no text ⚠️ [1].
- **Sally Box and Ayşin Turpancı** proposed three options on the climate finance work programme and Art. 2.1(c) (aligning finance flows with the Paris goals): substantive decisions, procedural decisions or no text ⚠️ [1].
- **On the Fund for loss and damage**, **the Arab Group** refuses to scale up resources without citing developed countries' obligation; **the EU, Switzerland and New Zealand** want to cite "all sources" of finance ⚠️ [1].
- **China and the Arab Group** want the guidance to the Adaptation Fund to recognise barriers caused by unilateral measures; **the EU and Switzerland** oppose ⚠️ [1].
- **Parties** reached consensus on the decision on least developed countries (LDCs) ⚠️ [1]. In another room the entire finance text remains in brackets, i.e. not agreed ✅ according to UN News [2].

## Relevance for the VSME
**Low** — No direct effect. The fight over unilateral measures (which include CBAM, the EU mechanism on the carbon content of imported goods) continues: CBAM rules remain in force.
