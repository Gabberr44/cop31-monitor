## In brief
**Article 6.2 of the Paris Agreement** lets countries trade emission reductions, called ITMOs (internationally transferred mitigation outcomes), to count towards their NDCs (national emission-reduction plans) ✅ [1]. **The dialogue** is a mandated technical event, not a negotiation, and produces no decisions ✅ [1].

## Key points
- **The UNFCCC Secretariat** set a single theme: the authorisation of ITMOs ✅ [1]. This is how a country decides whether, what and how much to authorise for an NDC or for other international mitigation purposes ✅ [1].
- **The concept note** sets guiding questions on links with multiple NDC implementation periods, long-term strategies and net zero targets ✅ [1]. It also asks how environmental integrity and safeguards are considered ✅ [1].
- **The format** is a roundtable: a 20' opening with Secretariat updates on Article 6 implementation, 60' of experience-sharing, 90' of moderated discussion ✅ [1].
- **The mandate** comes from decision 4/CMA.6 (para. 56) and decision 19/CMA.7 (paras. 18-19); the CMA is the meeting of the Parties to the Paris Agreement [1].
- **No source** gives a written account of what was said, even by the morning of 10 Nov: the ENB (Earth Negotiations Bulletin) could not be read (403 error) ⚠️.
- **co2news.sk** published previews of a Brazil–China deal and of funding for the 6.2 infrastructure: they remain unconfirmed, Tier C only ⚠️ [2].

## Relevance for the VSME
**Low** — **Authorisation rules** determine which credits are "counted" by a country and therefore cannot also be claimed by others. For an SME this only matters if it buys credits: in the VSME (EFRAG's voluntary standard for SMEs) they must be reported separately from gross emissions.
