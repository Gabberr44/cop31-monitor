# Health Day: Antalya Initiative for Resilient Health Systems (AIR)
✅ Final version — consolidated on 10/11/2026 at 07:30 (Italian time)

**When:** 09/11, time n/a — "Food, Agriculture and Health" thematic day ✅ [1][2] · **Where:** Antalya EXPO Center · **Organiser:** COP31 Presidency (Türkiye) with WHO (the World Health Organization) and ATACH (the Alliance for Transformative Action on Climate and Health) [2]
**Speakers (per Health Policy Watch, Tier C [3]):** **Kemal Memişoğlu** (Health Minister, Türkiye); **Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus** (Director-General, WHO); **Alexandre Padilha** (Health Minister, Brazil); **Tuafafa Latasi** (Health Minister, Tuvalu)
🎥 Video not yet available

## In brief
**The COP31 Presidency** lists "dynamic and resilient health systems" among the themes of its Action Agenda and devoted the first day to food, agriculture and health ✅ [1][2]. **The Presidency** launched AIR, a voluntary initiative on resilient health systems, with no funding announced yet ⚠️ [3].

## Key points
- **The COP31 Presidency, with WHO and ATACH**, launched the Antalya Initiative for Resilient Health Systems (AIR) ✅ [2].
- **Health Policy Watch** describes AIR as a voluntary mechanism: countries record and share what protected health from climate shocks and at what cost ⚠️ [3]. On Monday evening no funding, staff or participating countries had been announced; single source (Tier C) ⚠️ [3].
- **The COP31 Presidency** held high-level dialogues with women and young farmers, with IFAD (the International Fund for Agricultural Development) and FAO (the UN Food and Agriculture Organization) ✅ [2].
- **The COP31 Presidency** announces the launch of FAO's Turquoise Nexus Initiative for tomorrow ✅ [2]. The date conflicts with FAO's programme (see the daily summary).
- **Brazil** launched a financing framework for the Belém Health Action Plan, according to Health Policy Watch ⚠️ [3]. The plan lists about 60 non-binding measures backed by 40 countries ⚠️ [3].

## Announcements and numbers
- **Alexandre Padilha, Brazil's Health Minister**, said international public commitments for health adaptation fell from about USD 3.2 bn (2022) to USD 2.2 bn (2024), according to Health Policy Watch ⚠️ [3].
- **The COP31 Presidency**, citing official estimates, writes that the current "super" El Niño could push nearly 50 million more people into acute hunger ✅ [2].

## Relevance for the VSME
**Low** — **The initiative** has no direct effect on reporting. For SMEs, however, extreme heat is a physical risk to workers: it can feed into the qualitative climate-risk assessment and into VSME (EFRAG's voluntary standard for SMEs) disclosure B9 on health and safety.
