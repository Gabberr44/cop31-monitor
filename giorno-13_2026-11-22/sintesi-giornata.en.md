# COP31 — Day 13 · Sunday 22 November 2026
🕘 Provisional evening version — to be consolidated tomorrow morning at 09:30 (Italian time)<br>
⏱️ Third day of overtime and close of COP31: **the final decision (the "Antalya Pact") was not adopted** ✅ [1].

## In brief
- **What happened:** **Murat Kurum, COP31 President**, acknowledged in plenary that there was no consensus on the final decision, which was therefore not adopted ✅ [1].
- **Why it matters:** it is the first COP since 2009 (Copenhagen) to close without an overarching political deal, after the longest conference ever 🟡 [3][4][7].
- **Tomorrow:** COP31 is closed; the official texts of the technical decisions adopted are still to be published, while open issues move to Bonn (June 2027) and to COP32 in Addis Ababa ⚠️ [1][3].

## Key facts
1. **Murat Kurum, COP31 President and Turkish Environment Minister**, told the plenary there was no agreement on the text and the decisions were not adopted ✅ [1]. According to CNN he added: "We have done our utmost", but "there are parties in this hall who oppose this text" ⚠️ [4].<br>
   🎥 Video not yet available
2. **The European Union** raised a formal objection because the text contained no reference to fossil fuels ✅ [1]. **Darragh O'Brien, Irish Climate Minister, on behalf of the EU**, said the bloc would not endorse a deal "that does not even mention fossil fuels" 🟡 [4][8].
3. **The government of Panama** rejected the text and **the Marshall Islands** withheld support ✅ [1]. **Norway** also objected 🟡 [2][4]. According to Climate Home News, around 90 countries were opposed ⚠️ [2].
4. **Ghana, on behalf of the African Group (54 countries)**, said it would not block consensus despite the text's shortcomings ✅ [1]. **Saudi Arabia** defended the references to carbon capture and storage ✅ [1].
5. **António Guterres, UN Secretary-General**, called the outcome "deeply disappointing": "There may be no consensus in the negotiating room. There is no ambiguity in the science" ✅ [1].
6. **Simon Stiell, Executive Secretary of the UNFCCC (the UN climate convention)**, said "the world cannot afford a repeat of this moment" ✅ [1]. He cited as progress the just transition agreement and Action Agenda initiatives on electrification, resilience and investment ✅ [1].
7. **Chris Bowen, Australian minister and President of Negotiations**, called the outcome "very disappointing" 🟡 [4][5]. According to Bowen, the dealbreakers were fossil fuels and climate finance 🟡 [4][5].
