# How the COP works — a practical guide to COP31 in Antalya
Guide created on 16/11/2026 at 23:55 (Italian time)<br>
Versione italiana: [come-funziona-la-cop.md](come-funziona-la-cop.md) · Roadmap: [roadmap-cop31.en.md](roadmap-cop31.en.md)

This guide explains in plain terms how a UN climate conference works. It uses real COP31 examples taken from the daily summaries in this archive.

## 1. What the COP is
- **The COP (Conference of the Parties)** is the annual meeting of the countries that have joined the UNFCCC, the 1992 UN Framework Convention on Climate Change ✅ [1].
- **COP31** is being held in Antalya, Türkiye, from Monday 9 to Friday 20 November 2026 ✅ [1].
- **The COP can decide** common rules, work programmes, collective goals and guidance to the UN climate funds ✅ [1].
- **The COP cannot** impose obligations on an individual company. Nor can it write a country's policies into law: each state decides how to implement its commitments.
- **Why it matters:** COP decisions steer national plans, and national plans become laws that reach companies (see section 8).

## 2. Who decides: the Parties and the negotiating groups
**The Parties** are the states (plus the European Union) that have joined the Convention. They usually negotiate in groups to carry more weight. These are the main ones, with the rotating chair listed by the UNFCCC in November 2026 ✅ [5] and a typical position seen at COP31.

| Group | Who is in it | Typical position at COP31 |
|---|---|---|
| **European Union (EU)** | 27 member states; rotating chair: Ireland ✅ [5] | Wants the 1.5 °C overshoot mentioned and does not seek new mandates 🟡 [8]; defends strict carbon-credit rules 🟡 [9] |
| **G77 and China** | The large coalition of developing countries; chair: Uruguay ✅ [5] | Puts finance first, e.g. tripling adaptation finance 🟡 [9] |
| **Least Developed Countries (LDCs)** | The poorest countries; chair: Timor-Leste ✅ [5] | With the EU and island states, pushed for an ambitious mitigation work programme 🟡 [7] |
| **AOSIS (Alliance of Small Island States)** | The islands most exposed to sea-level rise; chair: Palau ✅ [5] | Calls for more ambition and resources for the Green Climate Fund 🟡 [8] |
| **African Group** | 54 African countries; chair: Ghana ✅ [5] | Opposes electrification targets without the funds to implement them 🟡 [8] |
| **Arab Group** | Arab countries; chair: Saudi Arabia ✅ [5] | Rejects global targets by sector or energy source 🟡 [7]; contests "unilateral" trade measures such as CBAM 🟡 [8] |
| **LMDC (Like-Minded Developing Countries)** | Including China, India, Saudi Arabia; spokesperson: China ✅ [5] | Said the draft final decision crosses its "red lines" ⚠️ [9] |
| **Umbrella Group** | Non-EU developed countries, including Australia, Canada, Japan, the UK; coordinator: UK ✅ [5] | With the EU and island states, defended transparency of emissions data 🟡 [7] |
| **AILAC** | An alliance of Latin American countries; chair: Panama ✅ [5] | With the EU and AOSIS, opposes weakening the adaptation goal 🟡 [9] |
| **EIG (Environmental Integrity Group)** | Switzerland, South Korea and others; chair: Switzerland ✅ [5] | Proposed COPs every 2-3 years and smaller delegations 🟡 [16] |

_Positions shift by topic: a country may side with one group on finance and with another on trade._

## 3. The bodies
- **COP:** the assembly of the 1992 Convention ✅ [1].
- **CMP:** the assembly of the Parties to the Kyoto Protocol; COP31 hosts its 21st session ✅ [1].
- **CMA:** the assembly of the Parties to the Paris Agreement; COP31 hosts its 8th session (CMA 8) ✅ [1]. This is where carbon markets, adaptation and transparency are decided.
- **SBI (Subsidiary Body for Implementation):** prepares decisions on implementation, finance and transparency 🟡 [7].
- **SBSTA (Subsidiary Body for Scientific and Technological Advice):** prepares technical and scientific decisions 🟡 [7].
- **The UNFCCC secretariat:** the Bonn-based UN office that organises the negotiations. It is led by **Simon Stiell, Executive Secretary**.

## 4. What is special about COP31: two countries in charge
- **COP30 in Belém (Brazil)** accepted **Türkiye**'s offer to host COP31 ✅ [1]. The same decision "welcomes" the arrangement between **Türkiye and Australia** ✅ [1].
- **Türkiye** hosts the conference and holds the presidency ✅ [2]. The COP31 President is **Murat Kurum, Turkish Minister of Environment** 🟡 [4].
- **Australia** chairs the negotiations ✅ [2]. **Chris Bowen, Australian Minister for Climate Change and Energy**, is "President of Negotiations" ✅ [6].
- **Bowen** said his role lets him manage the negotiations, prepare draft texts and issue the final decision 🟡 [3][4].
- **Murat Kurum** stated: "Türkiye and Australia are not co-presidents" and "the final decision lies with the presidency of COP31" 🟡 [4].
- **Türkiye** leads the Action Agenda, the non-negotiated part (thematic days, voluntary initiatives) 🟡 [4].
- **A Pacific island country** was to host the "Pre-COP", the preparatory ministerial meeting ✅ [1]. **Fiji**, with a leg in **Tuvalu**, hosted it in October 2026 🟡 [17].
- **Why it matters:** it is the first COP with powers split between two countries 🟡 [4]. Who has the last word on the final text remains an open question.

## 5. How a deal is reached
1. **Week one, technical work.** **The subsidiary bodies (SBI and SBSTA)** negotiate the technical texts. At COP31 they closed their 65th session overnight from Saturday 14 to Sunday 15 November 🟡 [7]. Many files moved to the CMA on non-agreed drafts 🟡 [7].
2. **Week two, political work.** Ministers arrive. **The Presidency** assigns open issues to pairs of ministers, one from a developed and one from a developing country 🟡 [7]. COP31 example: mitigation to **Carsten Schneider (Germany)** and **Bhupender Yadav (India)** 🟡 [7].
3. **Drafts.** Texts come out in successive versions. Passages in square brackets ("bracketed text") are not yet agreed. Alternative options stay in the text until someone gives way.
4. **The "cover decision".** This is the political text that accompanies the technical decisions. At COP31 **Chris Bowen** released the first draft on Monday 16 November 🟡 [10][11]. **Bowen** said: "It will not be the final draft" 🟡 [11]. In week one **the LMDC group** was already asking to avoid "unnecessary cover decisions" 🟡 [16].
5. **Consensus.** There is no vote at the COP: a decision passes if no Party present formally objects. That is why a single group can block a text.
6. **The closing plenary.** It is scheduled for Friday 20 November, the "Final Negotiations" day ✅ [6]. COPs often run over by one or two days.

## 6. Negotiations vs voluntary initiatives: what is binding
| Type | COP31 example | Binding? |
|---|---|---|
| Negotiated decision (COP/CMA) | Rules on Art. 6.4 carbon credits, discussed on 16/11 🟡 [9] | Yes for the states that adopt it, but only as a framework: companies are affected via national laws |
| Final "cover decision" | Draft with 35% electricity and USD 120 bn for adaptation 🟡 [10][11] | Politically important; usually creates no new legal obligations |
| Presidency voluntary pledge | "35-by-35": electricity at 35% of final energy use by 2035 ✅ [13] | No: opt-in |
| Voluntary pledge with UN agencies | Antalya AI Pledge, launched with the ITU on 16/11 ✅ [12] | No: voluntary commitments reviewed every year until 2030 ✅ [12] |
| Action Agenda | Presidency thematic days (e.g. "Finance and Trade", 13/11) ✅ [6] | No |

## 7. Blue Zone and Green Zone
- **The Blue Zone** is the UN-managed area: negotiating rooms, plenaries and country pavilions. Entry requires UNFCCC accreditation ✅ [14].
- **The Green Zone** is run by the host country and open to the public. At COP31 it was closed to visitors during the Leaders' Summit on 11-12 November ✅ [14].
- **Pavilions** are spaces for countries, organisations and companies. They host side events: panels, report launches, bilateral deals. They are not negotiations.

## 8. Why it matters for a company
The chain is long, but real:
1. **The COP** sets collective goals and common rules (for example on carbon markets).
2. **States** turn the goals into NDCs (national emission-reduction plans).
3. **The European Union and governments** make the laws. Examples: the Omnibus I directive, which narrowed the scope of the CSRD (the EU Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive) to companies with more than 1,000 employees and EUR 450 million turnover ✅ [15]; CBAM (the EU mechanism putting a carbon price on certain imports) ✅ [18].
4. **Larger companies, banks and customers** ask SMEs for data. Omnibus I protects companies with up to 1,000 employees: they can decline data requests that go beyond the voluntary standard ✅ [15]. That standard is the VSME (EFRAG's standard for SMEs).
5. **In practice:** an SME that measures energy and emissions (VSME, disclosure B3) is ready to answer customers, banks and importers. No COP decision on its own creates a direct obligation for an SME.

## 9. Glossary
- **AILAC:** Independent Association of Latin America and the Caribbean, a negotiating group.
- **AOSIS:** Alliance of Small Island States.
- **Art. 2.1(c):** the Paris Agreement article on aligning financial flows with climate goals.
- **Art. 6 / 6.2 / 6.4:** the Paris Agreement rules on carbon markets; 6.2 = trading between countries; 6.4 = the UN crediting mechanism.
- **BASIC:** Brazil, South Africa, India, China.
- **CBAM:** the EU Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism.
- **CMA / CMP / COP:** the assemblies of the Paris Agreement, the Kyoto Protocol and the Convention.
- **Cover decision:** the conference's overarching political decision.
- **CSRD / ESRS:** the EU sustainability reporting directive and its standards.
- **EFRAG:** the European body that writes reporting standards (ESRS and VSME).
- **EIG:** Environmental Integrity Group.
- **GGA:** Global Goal on Adaptation.
- **GHG:** greenhouse gases; **Scope 1, 2, 3:** direct emissions, emissions from purchased energy, value-chain emissions.
- **Global Stocktake (GST):** the Paris Agreement's five-yearly global review; the next one is in 2028.
- **G77 and China:** the coalition of developing countries.
- **IPCC:** the UN Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change.
- **ITMO:** emission reductions traded between countries under Art. 6.2.
- **ITU:** the International Telecommunication Union, the UN agency for digital technologies.
- **LDCs:** Least Developed Countries.
- **LMDC:** Like-Minded Developing Countries (including China and India).
- **MWP:** Mitigation Work Programme.
- **NDC:** Nationally Determined Contributions, national emission-reduction plans.
- **SME:** small and medium-sized enterprise.
- **SB / SBI / SBSTA:** the subsidiary bodies (for implementation and for scientific and technological advice).
- **UNEP:** the UN Environment Programme.
- **UNFCCC:** the UN Framework Convention on Climate Change and its secretariat.
- **VSME:** EFRAG's Voluntary Sustainability Reporting Standard for SMEs.
- **Blue Zone / Green Zone:** the UN negotiating area and the host country's public area.
