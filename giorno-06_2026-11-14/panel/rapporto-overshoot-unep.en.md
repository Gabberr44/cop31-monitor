# The UNEP 1.5 °C overshoot report under attack
**When:** consultations during the week of 9–14/11; article of 14/11 · **Where:** closed consultations on the final decision, Antalya EXPO Center · **Organiser:** COP31 Presidencies<br>
**Speakers:** **Laurence Tubiana** (CEO, European Climate Foundation); **Debra Roberts** (former co-chair of IPCC Working Group II); **Bill Hare** (CEO, Climate Analytics); **Manjeet Dhakal** (adviser to the Least Developed Countries)<br>
🎥 Video not planned (closed consultations)

## In brief
Some fossil-fuel producing countries are challenging UNEP's (UN Environment Programme) "Limiting Overshoot" report, published in September 2026. It matters because it decides whether the final decision will reference 1.5 °C overshoot and the latest science on impacts.

## Key points
- **The Arab Group, led by Saudi Arabia**, questioned the report's scientific rigour, arguing it was not peer-reviewed ⚠️ according to Climate Home, citing anonymous sources [1].
- **The LMDCs (Like-Minded Developing Countries, including China and India)** want the final decision to avoid references to reports produced outside the UN climate process ⚠️ [1].
- **The EU, UK, Least Developed Countries and small island states** want the response to 1.5 °C overshoot to be a COP31 priority ⚠️ [1].
- **Debra Roberts, former IPCC co-chair**, explains that the authors followed the global-assessment procedure, with external reviewers; only governments did not review the text ⚠️ [1].
- **The IPCC (Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change)** will hold an online meeting in early December on the timeline of its reports for the 2028 Global Stocktake ⚠️ [1].

## Announcements and numbers
- **The UNEP report** has 44 authors and 44 reviewers, in a year-long exercise ⚠️ [1].

## Quotes
- "[The report] tells us a truth some would rather hide" — **Laurence Tubiana, European Climate Foundation** [1]
- "The reports are being attacked because some people don't like the conclusions" — **Bill Hare, Climate Analytics** [1]
