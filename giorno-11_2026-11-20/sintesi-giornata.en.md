# COP31 — Day 11 · Friday 20 November 2026
✅ Final version — consolidated on 21/11/2026 at 09:48 (Italian time)<br>
⏱️ COP31 remains in overtime on Saturday 21/11: at consolidation no third draft of the final decision had been published 🟡 [15].

Theme of the day chosen by the **COP31 Presidency (Türkiye)**: "Final Negotiations" ✅ [1].

## In brief
- **What happened:** on the last scheduled day **the COP31 Presidency** did not publish a new draft ✅ [3]. **Two coalitions of countries** want the transition away from fossil fuels back in the text ✅ [3] 🟡 [6][11].
- **Why it matters:** without consensus no decision passes; a single country, such as **Panama**, can block the final deal ✅ [3].
- **Tomorrow:** work continues on Saturday 21; the third draft did not appear overnight 🟡 [15].

## Key facts
1. **The COP31 Presidency** did not publish a new version of the final decision (the "cover decision", called the "Antalya Accords") by Friday ✅ [3]. No new draft appeared overnight either: Thursday 19/11's text is still on the table 🟡 [2][15].<br>
   🎥 Video not yet available (re-checked on 21/11/2026)
2. **The closing plenary** met late in the evening, well past the official closing time, and approved only some technical items ⚠️ according to AFP [5][15]. According to **Hürriyet Daily News**, the decisions adopted include a text on cooperation under Article 6 of the Paris Agreement (carbon markets) ⚠️ [17]. The plenary was suspended until Saturday morning ⚠️ [18].
3. **Murat Kurum, COP31 President and Turkish Environment Minister**, said: "We are close to the finish line, but we are not there yet" ⚠️ according to AFP [5]. He called for closing with an "Antalya Consensus" ⚠️ [17]. **Chris Bowen, Australian Climate Minister and President of Negotiations**, will work "for as long as it takes" ⚠️ according to AFP [5]. For Bowen, "electrification is the key to transitioning away from fossil fuels" ⚠️ [15].
4. **Nine countries led by France and Panama** (with Belgium, Côte d'Ivoire, Finland, Greece, Guatemala, Spain, Sweden and Vanuatu) said the draft is "not good enough" ✅ [3]. They ask for six priorities, including a "clear and orderly" transition away from fossil fuels ✅ [3].
5. **Ministers and envoys from 23 countries**, led by **Stientje van Veldhoven, Dutch Climate Minister**, want the "transition away from fossil fuels" back in the text ⚠️ according to Carbon Brief [6]. They include Germany, the United Kingdom, Brazil, Indonesia, Norway and Pacific countries ⚠️ [6].
6. **China and Australia** signed a joint statement on climate and the energy transition ✅ [8]. They welcome "on a voluntary basis" the goal of 35% electricity in final energy consumption by 2035 ✅ [8]. For **Carbon Brief** it is China's clearest support yet for the "35 by 35" goal ⚠️ [7].
7. **The European Union** warns that the draft "risks turning this climate COP into a fossil summit" ⚠️ according to AFP [5]. **Irish broadcaster RTÉ** reports testy exchanges in the evening between the EU and the two Presidencies ⚠️ [16]. **Saudi Arabia and the Arab Group** argue that each country should decide its own energy mix ⚠️ [16]. **Juan Carlos Navarro, Panama's Environment Minister**, accuses a "petroleum mafia" of blocking progress ⚠️ according to AFP [15].

_Correction:_ **the Global SF6 Pledge** (a voluntary commitment on sulphur hexafluoride, the most potent greenhouse gas) was presented on Thursday 19/11, not 20/11, by **Carsten Schneider, German Environment Minister** ✅ [19]. Supporters include the European Union, France, Spain, the United Kingdom, Canada and Kenya ✅ [19].
