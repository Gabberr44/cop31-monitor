# China–Australia climate statement
**When:** 20/11, published during the day, time n.a. · **Where:** written statement (released from Beijing, Xinhua agency) · **Organiser:** governments of China and Australia<br>
**Speakers:** no oral remarks; joint text of the two governments<br>
🎥 Not applicable (written text)

## In brief
**China and Australia** published a joint statement on climate change and the clean energy transition ✅ [1]. It matters because China clearly backs, for the first time, the COP31 Presidency's electrification goal ⚠️ according to Carbon Brief [2].

## Key points
- **The two governments** welcome "on a voluntary basis" the goal of raising electricity to 35% of global final energy consumption by 2035 ✅ [1].
- **The two governments** support tripling renewables and doubling the rate of energy efficiency improvement by 2030 ✅ [1].
- **The two governments** commit to keep 1.5 °C "within reach" and to minimise the magnitude and duration of any overshoot ✅ [1].
- **The two governments** cite Article 3.5 of the UN climate Convention: climate measures must not be "a disguised restriction on international trade" ✅ [1].
- **The statement** provides for bilateral exchanges on climate transparency and a dialogue on steel decarbonisation, launched on 02/11/2026 ✅ [1].
- **Simon Evans, of Carbon Brief**, notes the text mentions neither finance nor fossil fuels ⚠️ [2].

## Relevance for the VSME
**Medium** — electrification becomes a goal shared by China too; Article 3.5 is the argument used against measures such as CBAM (the EU Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism), which nonetheless stays in force.
