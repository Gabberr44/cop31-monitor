# Coalitions for a fossil-fuel exit
**When:** 20/11, morning and afternoon, times n.a. · **Where:** Antalya EXPO Center, press rooms · **Organiser:** the Netherlands; France and Panama<br>
**Speakers:** **Stientje van Veldhoven** (Dutch climate minister); **Monique Barbut** (French minister for ecological transition); **Juan Carlos Navarro** (Panama's environment minister)<br>
🎥 Video not yet available (re-checked on 21/11/2026)

## In brief
Two groups of countries want the transition away from fossil fuels, dropped from the second draft, back in the final decision ✅ [1] ⚠️ [2]. It matters because under consensus a single formal objection can stop the deal ✅ [1].

## Key points
- **Nine countries led by France and Panama** (Belgium, Côte d'Ivoire, Finland, Greece, Guatemala, Spain, Sweden, Vanuatu) say the draft is "not good enough" ✅ [1] 🟡 [3].
- **Their six priorities** are: science, a "clear and orderly" transition away from fossil fuels, finance from the largest emitters, clean electrification, methane cuts, cooperation among the three Rio Conventions ✅ [1].
- **Stientje van Veldhoven, Dutch climate minister**, spoke on behalf of 23 countries, including Germany, the UK, Brazil, Indonesia, Norway, Switzerland and Pacific countries ⚠️ according to Carbon Brief [2].
- **Monique Barbut, French minister**, wants the "Antalya Accord" to include "clean electrification and transitioning away from fossil fuels" ⚠️ according to Carbon Brief [2].
- **Juan Carlos Navarro, Panama's minister**, called a COP text without fossil fuels "not acceptable" ✅ [1] 🟡 [4]. He urged everyone to sign the "35 by 35" electrification pledge ⚠️ [2].
- **Ralph Regenvanu, Vanuatu's climate minister**, said the text is "not going to be a high-ambition text" ⚠️ according to Reuters [4].

## Quotes
- "You pollute; you pay." — **Juan Carlos Navarro, Panama's environment minister** [1]

## Relevance for the VSME
**Low** — no direct effect on SMEs; an explicit reference to fossil fuels would however strengthen national exit roadmaps, affecting energy costs and the transition plans banks ask for.
