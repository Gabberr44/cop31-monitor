# COP31 — Day 8 · Tuesday 17 November 2026
🕘 Provisional evening version — to be consolidated tomorrow morning at 09:30 (Italian time)<br>
First draft: 17/11/2026 at 22:45 (Italian time).

Theme of the day chosen by the **COP31 Presidency (Türkiye)**: "Ocean, Seas, Nature and Land Use" ✅ [1]. The resumed high-level segment began, with ministers' statements (17–18 November) ✅ [11].

## At a glance
- **What happened:** **Wopke Hoekstra, EU Climate Commissioner**, called the draft final decision a "shopping list" that needs significant change 🟡 [3][4]. The first closed-door ministerial consultations began ⚠️ [2].
- **Why it matters:** three days before the close, technical talks have settled almost nothing; the knots move to ministers ⚠️ [2].
- **Tomorrow:** on Wednesday 18 ministers' statements continue ✅ [11]; at least one more draft of the final decision is expected by Friday ⚠️ according to AFP [4].

## Key facts
1. **Wopke Hoekstra, European Commissioner for Climate**, told ministers the draft has "many of the characteristics of a shopping list" and "needs significant change" 🟡 [3][4]. According to AFP, he warned it could make the process "untenable" ⚠️ [4]. **Chris Bowen, COP31 President of Negotiations (Australia)**, acknowledged the text contains elements "that not all parties agree on" and announced "at least one more draft" ⚠️ according to AFP [4].
2. **The European Union** backs the voluntary goal of raising electricity to 35% of final energy use by 2035 ("35-by-35") 🟡 [2][3]. **Hoekstra** said: "The world needs much quicker, much more radical electrification" ⚠️ according to Climate Home [3]. **The Arab Group**, by contrast, rejected references to roadmaps, "top-down" sectoral targets and the International Court of Justice advisory opinion ⚠️ according to ENB [2].
3. **Denmark** became the fifth country to present a national roadmap away from fossil fuels, after Germany, France, the Netherlands and Finland ⚠️ according to Climate Home [3]. **Samira Nawa, Danish Minister for Climate and Energy**, raised the 2035 target to "at least" an 85% emissions cut, up from 82% ⚠️ [3]. **Spain, Portugal, Ireland, Luxembourg and the Pacific countries** have committed to publishing theirs ⚠️ [3].
4. **Ilana Seid, chair of AOSIS (the Alliance of Small Island States)**, called any delay to the next IPCC (the UN climate science panel) reports "unjustifiable" 🟡 [3][4]. **Saudi Arabia's delegation** replied that it is not seeking to delay science but wants adequate review timelines 🟡 [3][5].
5. **Gilberto Pichetto Fratin, Italy's Minister of Environment and Energy Security**, told the plenary that "climate action also requires adequate resources" ⚠️ according to Radiocor [8]. He cited EUR 5.8 billion of Italian public climate finance in 2022–2025, plus EUR 3.2 billion mobilised ⚠️ [8]. With **SEforALL (Sustainable Energy for All)** he presented the CLEAR Fund, an Italian fund based on Article 6.2 (trading of reductions between countries) ⚠️ [9]. **Marica Di Pierri, spokesperson for the NGO A Sud**, criticised the minister for not saying how and when Italy will exit fossil fuels ⚠️ according to ANSA [10].
6. **UN News (UN)** reports that more than 95% of coastal and island countries include ocean measures in their NDCs (national emission-reduction plans) ✅ [6]. About 60% of these depend on external finance or support ✅ [6]. **The European Union** joined the Blue NDC Challenge led by France and Brazil ✅ [6].
7. **Germanwatch (a German NGO)** presented the Climate Risk Index 2027: Dominica, Myanmar and Honduras are the countries hardest hit by extreme events over the past 30 years ✅ according to UN News [6]. From 1996 to 2025 extreme events caused more than 916,000 deaths ✅ [6]. **23 countries** endorsed the Antalya Action Plan on Integrated Fire Management, backed by FAO (the UN food agency) ✅ [6].
8. **Mafalda Duarte, Executive Director of the Green Climate Fund (GCF)**, asked rich countries to lead the fund's 2028–2031 replenishment ⚠️ according to Climate Home [7]. In October the GCF board approved accepting contributions from foundations and carbon markets ⚠️ [7].

## Negotiations: where we stand
| Theme | What moved | What is stuck | Texts released |
|---|---|---|---|
| Final decision ("cover decision") | Consultations led by **Sally Box (Presidency of Negotiations)**; **AOSIS** and the **Environmental Integrity Group (EIG)** call for transitioning away from fossil fuels ⚠️ [2] | **Arab Group**: no to roadmaps and sectoral targets ⚠️ [2]; **EU**: text too broad 🟡 [3][4] | No new draft; at least one more expected ⚠️ [4] |
| Mitigation / NDCs | **EU**: support for 35% electricity by 2035 🟡 [2][3] | Periodic review of the long-term goal: three options still open ⚠️ [2] | New iteration of the periodic-review text ⚠️ [2] |
| Article 6 (carbon markets) | Revised text on 6.2 (trading between countries); **EU**: stricter conflict-of-interest rules in 6.4 ⚠️ [2] | Fees to fund registries and reviews: countries split on exemptions ⚠️ [2] | Revised drafts on 6.2, 6.4, 6.8 ⚠️ [2] |
| Finance | **EU**: a biennial report on Art. 2.1(c) (aligning finance flows) ⚠️ [2] | **LDCs (Least Developed Countries), Arab Group, AILAC (an alliance of Latin American countries)** opposed ⚠️ [2]; finance work programme: **EU and Switzerland** want no text ⚠️ [3] | Second iteration of the Standing Committee on Finance drafts ⚠️ [2] |
| Adaptation / GGA (Global Goal on Adaptation) | **Solomon Islands and Fiji**: tripling finance is being handled in the final decision ⚠️ [2] | USD 120 billion baseline demanded by developing countries ⚠️ [3] | The Presidency will prepare a revised text ⚠️ [2] |
| Just transition | Contact group co-chaired by **Federica Fricano (Italy)** and **Joseph Teo (Singapore)** ⚠️ [2] | **China**: a dedicated body; for the **EU** a "red line" ⚠️ [2] | Informal note ⚠️ [2] |
