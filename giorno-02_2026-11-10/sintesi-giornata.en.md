# COP31 — Day 2 · Tuesday 10 November 2026
✅ Final version — consolidated on 11/11/2026 at 07:45 (Italian time)

## In brief
- **What happened:** **The Turkish COP31 Presidency** devoted the day to energy and transport and launched the "35-by-35" electrification package ✅ [1][9].
- **Why it matters:** banks and large customers may ask SMEs more often for their share of electricity and renewables in energy use, a data point the VSME (EFRAG's voluntary standard for SMEs) already covers.
- **Tomorrow:** **the Antalya Leaders' Summit** opens with leaders' statements and the "Zero Waste" thematic day ✅ [11].

## Key facts
1. **The Turkish COP31 Presidency** presented the "35-by-35" package ✅ [1]. The voluntary goal raises electricity from about 23% to 35% of final consumption by 2035 ✅ [1]. The package creates an Electrification Forum with the European Union and an Electrification Board with the IEA (International Energy Agency) as secretariat ✅ [1]. It also sets up an IEA-led tracking mechanism ✅ [1]. The Electrify Now Finance Coalition is launched, with the World Bank and the African Development Bank as co-chairs from 2027 ✅ [1]. **Murat Kurum, COP31 President**, said: "COP31 will be remembered as the moment we flicked the switch" ✅ [1].
2. **Some fossil fuel producing countries** are expected to oppose any reference to "clean" electricity in the final decision 🟡 [7][8]. The fight stems from the text: it does not require the additional electricity to come from clean sources 🟡 [8][15]. **Chris Bowen, President of Negotiations (Australia)**, said that without 35% the world "has no chance of holding to 1.5 degrees" 🟡 [8].
3. **The Global Carbon Project**, in the Global Carbon Budget 2026 report, puts fossil CO₂ emissions at 38.2 bn t, down 0.4% ✅ [3]. It is the first fall since 2020, partly due to the oil shock linked to the Iran conflict ✅ [3]. Total emissions, however, rise to 43.3 bn t ✅ [3]. The remaining 1.5 °C budget is 150 bn t, under four years of emissions ✅ [3].
4. **The COP30 Presidency (Brazil)** presented its TAFF (transition away from fossil fuels) roadmap in plenary 🟡 [14][15]. The roadmap sets national plans by 2028 and supply cuts from 2031 🟡 [14][15]. **António Guterres, UN Secretary-General**, said: "It is long past time to stop treating the transition away from fossil fuels as taboo or intractable" 🟡 [14][15]. **Governments** have no shared plan, and COP31 has no agenda item on fossil fuels 🟡 [8][15]. **India, Nigeria and Germany** backed the roadmap, according to Climate Home ⚠️ [14].
5. **UNEZA (Utilities for Net Zero Alliance, the alliance of power companies for net zero)** raises its annual commitment for grids, renewables and storage from USD 148 bn to USD 156 bn ✅ [1]. **The We Mean Business Coalition** brings together over 180 companies, with USD 1.69 tn in revenue, that back electrification ✅ [1].
6. **Australia** leads a declaration to double the number of households with rooftop solar to 300 million by 2030 🟡 [7][8]. **Bangladesh, Kenya, Nigeria and Vanuatu** are among the signatories 🟡 [7][8].
7. **The United States** is absent for the second year running 🟡 [15][16]. **Carbon Brief** counts 92,934 in-person registrants (provisional figures): it would be the largest COP ever ⚠️ [17]. According to Carbon Brief, the largest delegation is Türkiye's (2,950), followed by Brazil, Nigeria, China and Indonesia ⚠️ [17]. **Climate Home News** instead reports 68,374 in-person registrants, of whom 14,285 are host-country guests (about one in five) ⚠️ [7]. These guests include fossil fuel executives, reported by a single source ⚠️ [7].
