# Energy and Transport Day: the "35-by-35" electrification package
**When:** 10/11, all day, with a Presidency press conference; Global Electrification Ministerial 17:30–19:30 Antalya (15:30–17:30 Italian time) according to the European Commission [2] · **Where:** Antalya EXPO Center, Blue Zone · **Organiser:** COP31 Presidency (Türkiye) with the Presidency of Negotiations (Australia); Electrification Forum co-hosted with the European Union [1]
**Speakers** (quoted by the Presidency [1]): **Murat Kurum** (President, COP31 – Türkiye); **Chris Bowen** (COP31 President of Negotiations, Australian Minister for Climate Change and Energy); **Fatih Birol** (Executive Director, IEA – International Energy Agency); **Francesco La Camera** (Director-General, IRENA – International Renewable Energy Agency); **Martin Pibworth** (CEO, SSE plc; co-chair of UNEZA – Utilities for Net Zero Alliance, the alliance of power companies for net zero); **Bruce Douglas** (CEO, Global Renewables Alliance); **Damilola Ogunbiyi** (CEO, SEforALL – Sustainable Energy for All); **Mathias Cormann** (Secretary-General, OECD – Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development); **Maria Mendiluce** (CEO, We Mean Business Coalition)

## In brief
**The Turkish COP31 Presidency** presented the "35-by-35 Global Electrification Pledge" on the "Energy and Transport" thematic day: it is the flagship of its Action Agenda (the agenda of voluntary initiatives), first announced in Bonn in June [1][3]. It matters because tracking electrification and a dedicated finance coalition may lead to new requests for energy data from companies.

## Key points
- **The COP31 Presidency** sets a voluntary goal: electricity from about 23% to 35% of final energy consumption by 2035 ✅ [1]. The goal covers transport, buildings and industry and is based on IEA and IRENA analysis ✅ [1].
- **The COP31 Presidency** creates a five-part delivery architecture ✅ [1]:
  - an **Electrification Forum** with the EU, continuing after the COP;
  - an **Electrification Board** chaired by the Presidency, with the IEA as secretariat;
  - a **Global Electrification Tracking Mechanism** led by the IEA;
  - IRENA's **D-GRIDS** programme for distribution grids;
  - the **Global Green Freight Corridors Initiative** with the International Transport Forum.
- **The Electrify Now Finance Coalition** brings together governments, development banks and private finance for emerging economies ✅ [1]. **The Presidency** names the World Bank and the African Development Bank as inaugural co-chairs in 2027 ✅ [1]. **Climate Home News** writes that the coalition is backed by Türkiye and Ethiopia (COP32 host) ⚠️ [3]. According to Climate Home, the EBRD (European Bank for Reconstruction and Development), the EIB (European Investment Bank) and the IDB (Inter-American Development Bank) have joined ⚠️ [3]. These names come from a single source; the Presidency's account is the official one.
- **Some fossil fuel producing countries** are expected to oppose any reference to clean electricity in the final decision 🟡 [3][4]. This is the political sticking point: the text does not require the additional electricity to be clean or renewable 🟡 [3][4].
- **Climate Home News** reports that Australia, Ethiopia, Germany and Norway have joined so far ⚠️ [3]. According to Climate Home, no full list is expected this week ⚠️ [3]. A **Climate Action Tracker** analysis projects 28% under current policies, reported by a single source ⚠️ [3].
- **Australia** leads the Global Rooftop Solar Declaration: double the number of households with rooftop solar to 300 million by 2030 🟡 [3][4]. **Bangladesh, Kenya, Nigeria and Vanuatu** are among the signatories; the pledge is non-binding 🟡 [3][4].

## Announcements and numbers
- **UNEZA** raises its annual investment commitment in grids, renewables and storage from USD 148 bn to USD 156 bn ✅ [1].
- **The We Mean Business Coalition** brings together more than 180 companies that back electrification, with USD 1.69 trillion in combined annual revenue ✅ [1]. They include Volvo, Uber, IKEA and Siemens ✅ [1].
- **The IEA, IRENA, SEforALL and the OECD** publish new reports ✅ [1]:
  - IEA: Electrification Special Report;
  - IRENA: a roadmap;
  - SEforALL: "The Electrification Opportunity", with Türkiye and Australia;
  - OECD: two notes on financing and economic opportunities.
- **The Marrakech Partnership (the UN partnership for climate action)** presents the 2026 Yearbook of Global Climate Action ✅ [1]. It counts 83 cooperative initiatives and over 200 actions ✅ [1]. It records progress in 84% of the "Plans to Accelerate Solutions" ✅ [1].
- **The Climate Investment Funds** announced nearly USD 100 m to de-risk clean power, grid and storage projects, according to Climate Home ⚠️ [3]. The aim is to mobilise over USD 1.2 bn of private finance, reported by a single source ⚠️ [3].
- **The COP31 Presidency** announces the next thematic days: Resilient Cities (12 Nov), Finance (13 Nov), Industry (16 Nov) ✅ [1].

## Quotes
- "The age of electricity is taking shape – and COP31 will be remembered as the moment we flicked the switch." — **Murat Kurum, COP31 President** [1]
- "The world has no chance of holding to 1.5 degrees unless we lift that to 35 per cent." — **Chris Bowen, President of Negotiations**, according to ABC [4]

## Relevance for the VSME
**Medium** — no obligations for companies, but IEA tracking and the finance coalition make it more likely that banks and large customers will ask SMEs for the share of electricity (and of renewables) in energy use, a data point the VSME (EFRAG's voluntary standard for SMEs) already covers in module B3.
