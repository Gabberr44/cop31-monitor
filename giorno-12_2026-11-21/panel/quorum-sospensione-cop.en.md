# Quorum: what it takes to suspend COP31
**When:** 21/11, afternoon and evening (written discussion) · **Where:** Carbon Brief Connect (online forum) · **Organiser:** Carbon Brief<br>
**Speakers:** **Giuliana Viglione** (Carbon Brief); **Paul Watkinson** and **Farhana Yamin** (climate-negotiation experts, forum contributions); **Simon Evans** (Carbon Brief)<br>
🎥 Not applicable (written text)

## In brief
Carbon Brief explains when a COP loses the minimum number of countries present and must be suspended ⚠️ [1]. It matters because, as delegates leave, COP31 risks closing without a political decision ⚠️ [1].

## Key points
- **The UNFCCC rules of procedure** (still in draft form, but applied) require two thirds of the Parties to be present to take decisions ⚠️ according to Carbon Brief [1].
- **Carbon Brief's calculation:** 132 of the Convention's 198 Parties are needed, or 130 of the Paris Agreement's 194; the United States technically remains a Party ⚠️ [1].
- **Paul Watkinson** recalls that COP6 in The Hague (2000) was suspended over disagreement on substance, not quorum, and resumed in Bonn in July 2001 ⚠️ [1].
- **Carbon Brief** cites the CBD COP16 biodiversity meeting in Cali (2024): **Panama** called a quorum check, which failed, and the meeting resumed in Rome in February ⚠️ [1].
- **Simon Evans** confirms that in Madrid (COP25, 2019) quorum was discussed but never tested ⚠️ [1].

## Relevance for the VSME
**Low** — a suspension would push political decisions to 2027, with no immediate effect on SMEs' reporting obligations.
