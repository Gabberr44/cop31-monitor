## In brief
**The UN Early Warnings for All initiative** held a high-level event and released a progress report ✅ [1]. **The report** shows that warning systems save lives, but one country in three still has none ✅ [1].

## Key points
- **The report** counts 136 countries (70%) with multi-hazard early warning systems, 17 more than in March 2025 ✅ [1]
- **The report** finds disaster mortality 6.1 times lower where systems are substantial or comprehensive, compared with countries with limited systems ✅ [1]
- **About one country in three** still has no system ✅ [1]. **The biggest gaps** are in least developed countries, small islands and conflict-affected countries ✅ [1]
- **António Guterres, UN Secretary-General**, sets out three priorities ✅ [1]:
  - make early warning a permanent public service;
  - close finance and capacity gaps;
  - move from fragmented projects to coordinated, long-term support for national plans.
- **The WMO (World Meteorological Organization)** warns the current El Niño could exceed existing monitoring capacity ✅ [1]

## Announcements and numbers
- **The initiative** has mobilised about USD 2 bn since 2022 ✅ [1]
- **About 160 countries** are receiving support ✅ [1]
- **In 2025, 146 anticipatory action activations** in 54 countries reached 9.6 million people ✅ [1]

## Quotes
- "No one should lose their life simply because a warning came too late – or never came at all." — **António Guterres, UN Secretary-General** [1]
