# Leaders' summit on methane and non-CO₂ greenhouse gases
**When:** 12/11, 09:00–10:30 Antalya (07:00–08:30 Italian time) · **Where:** Meeting Room-3 (Topkapı) · **Organiser:** United Kingdom in the official programme ✅ [1]; co-hosted by China, the United Kingdom and Brazil ⚠️ [3]<br>
**Speakers:** **Monique Barbut** (Minister for Ecological Transition, France); **Mia Mottley** (Prime Minister, Barbados); **Ana Toni** (National Secretary for Climate Change, Brazil); **Heike Henn** (State Secretary, Germany); **Chris Bowen** (Climate Minister, Australia, and COP31 President of Negotiations) [2][3]<br>
🎥 Video not yet available

## In brief
Leaders called for stronger rules on methane, a gas that warms far more than CO₂ in the short term. Private donors pledged new money, while some governments push to mention methane in the COP31 final decision.

## Key points
- **Philanthropic donors** pledged more than USD 480 million to cut methane in energy, agriculture and waste ✅ [3][4]. A further USD 25 million goes to emission cuts in seven countries ✅ [4]. This adds to more than USD 530 million delivered since 2021 ⚠️ [3].
- **Monique Barbut, French minister**, asked for a strong mention of methane in the final decision ("cover decision") ⚠️ according to Climate Home and Health Policy Watch [2][3]. She wants "near-zero" oil and gas methane intensity by 2030 and an end to flaring and venting (burning and direct release of gas) ⚠️ [2][3].
- **Mia Mottley, Prime Minister of Barbados**, proposed a binding global methane agreement instead of voluntary pledges ⚠️ [2][3].
- **Ana Toni, Brazil's climate secretary**, said methane emissions "are flat" ⚠️ according to Health Policy Watch [3].
- **Chris Bowen, Australian Climate Minister**, said Australia will sign a voluntary "near-zero" methane pledge for fossil fuels ⚠️ according to Climate Home [2]. According to Health Policy Watch he did not mention methane in his remarks at the summit ⚠️ [3].

## Announcements and figures
- **Health Policy Watch**: tracked methane-cutting finance fell from USD 7.7 billion (2021) to USD 5.2 billion (2023), about 4% of what is needed ⚠️ [3].
- **Climate Home**: about 40% of methane comes from agriculture, over 20% from landfills and a third from oil and gas 🟡 [2].

## Quotes
- "This cover decision must incorporate a strong and substantive mention to methane." — **Monique Barbut, French minister** [2]

## Relevance for the VSME
**Medium** — In the VSME (module B3) methane is part of Scope 1 emissions in tCO₂e: SMEs with gas boilers, network leaks, livestock or wastewater may get more precise questions from customers and banks.
