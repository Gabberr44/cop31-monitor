# Clean cooking in Africa: leaders' roundtable
**When:** 12/11, 14:30–16:00 Antalya (12:30–14:00 Italian time) · **Where:** Leaders' Summit venue · **Organiser:** Türkiye, Tanzania, IEA (International Energy Agency) ✅ [1]<br>
**Speakers:** **Ali Mohamed** (Climate Envoy, Kenya); **Nthomeng Majara** (Deputy Prime Minister, Lesotho); **Jiwoh Emmanuel Abdulai** (Environment Minister, Sierra Leone) [2]<br>
🎥 Video not yet available

## In brief
African governments called for closing the funding gap for clean cooking, i.e. stoves and fuels that replace wood and charcoal. Part of the proposed solution relies on carbon credits.

## Key points
- **Ali Mohamed, Kenya's Climate Envoy**, announced that Nairobi will host the second global clean cooking summit in January ⚠️ according to Climate Home [2].
- **The IEA** estimates Africa needs USD 4 billion a year until 2030; up to USD 2.2 billion was pledged at the 2024 Paris summit ⚠️ according to Climate Home [2].
- **Nthomeng Majara, Deputy Prime Minister of Lesotho**, called for grant-based and concessional finance ⚠️ [2].
- **Jiwoh Emmanuel Abdulai, Sierra Leone's Environment Minister**, said investors need high-integrity carbon markets ⚠️ [2].
- **BURN (cookstove developer)** announced deals with Togo and Nigeria to sell Article 6 carbon credits (the Paris Agreement mechanism for trading credits between countries) ⚠️ [2].

## Relevance for the VSME
**Low** — Clean-cookstove credits are among the most common on the voluntary market. An SME buying them cannot subtract them from its B3 emissions: they must be reported separately.
