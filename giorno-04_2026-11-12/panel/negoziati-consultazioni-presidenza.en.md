# Day 4 negotiations: Presidency consultations and technical drafts
**When:** 12/11, all day · **Where:** negotiating rooms, Antalya EXPO Center · **Organiser:** COP31 Presidency (Türkiye), Presidency of Negotiations (Australia) and UNFCCC subsidiary bodies [1]<br>
**Speakers:** **Kushla Munro** (Co-Facilitator, COP31 Presidency of Negotiations); **Ayşin Turpancı** (Co-Facilitator, COP31 Presidency); **Chao Feng** (Co-Facilitator, COP31 Presidency of Negotiations); **Pacifica F. Achieng Ogola** (Kenya) and **Peer Stiansen** (Norway), Co-Facilitators on Article 6.2; **Georg Børsting** (Norway), Co-Chair on finance [1]<br>
🎥 Informal sessions, no recording planned

## In brief
While leaders spoke in plenary, negotiators discussed drafts on UN process reform, carbon markets, finance and adaptation. It matters for business because this is where the costs and rules of carbon credits and the role of private finance are decided.

## Key points
- **Environmental Integrity Group (EIG: Switzerland, South Korea, Mexico and others)** proposed COPs every two to three years, caps on delegation size and an independent review of the process 🟡 [1].
- **LMDC group (Like-Minded Developing Countries)** asked to avoid "unnecessary cover decisions" 🟡 [1]. **Japan** asked the Secretariat to remove duplicated activities, citing the departure of the historically largest contributor 🟡 [1].
- **The European Union** wants usage-based fees to fund the technical review under Article 6.2 (bilateral trading of carbon credits) 🟡 [1]. **The LMDCs** want waivers for all developing countries 🟡 [1]. The draft goes to the CMA (the meeting of the Parties to the Paris Agreement) 🟡 [1].
- **New Zealand, Canada, the UK, Norway, Switzerland, South Korea and Japan** want private finance mobilisation in the climate finance work programme 🟡 [1]. **LMDCs, the Arab Group and AOSIS (small island states)** oppose 🟡 [1].
- **The Arab Group** refused to acknowledge the Veredas Dialogue report on Article 2.1(c), i.e. aligning financial flows with climate goals 🟡 [1].
- **The Arab Group and the African Group (54 countries)** called unilateral trade measures inconsistent with the Convention's principles 🟡 [1]. The implicit reference is to tools such as CBAM (the EU Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism).

## Announcements and figures
- **Co-facilitators on adaptation communications**: Party submissions by September 2027, synthesis report to CMA 9 (November 2027) 🟡 [1].

## Relevance for the VSME
**Medium** — Article 6 rules and costs affect the price and quality of carbon credits an SME might buy. The debate on unilateral measures directly concerns SMEs exporting CBAM-covered products to the EU.
