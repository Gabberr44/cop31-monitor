# The Antalya Pledge on artificial intelligence
**When:** 16/11, 09:00–11:00 Antalya (07:00–09:00 Italian time) · **Where:** Special Event Room No. 2 · **Organiser:** COP31 Presidency and ITU (International Telecommunication Union, the UN digital agency)<br>
**Speakers:** **Tomas Lamanauskas** (ITU Deputy Secretary-General, chair); **Simon Stiell** (UNFCCC Executive Secretary); **Kushla Munro** (Australia); **Mehmet Fatih Kacır** (Türkiye's Minister of Industry and Technology); **Dirk Messner** (German Environment Agency); **Evan Tachovsky** (World Resources Institute) — official programme, order subject to change [2]<br>
🎥 [Full video — High-level Roundtable on AI (UNFCCC Global Climate Action YouTube channel)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5sDPFX--RVY)

## At a glance
The COP31 Presidency and the ITU launched a voluntary pledge to reduce the climate footprint of artificial intelligence (AI). One of its four pillars is measuring and reporting AI's environmental impact with standard methods: a topic that will also reach supply chains.

## Key points
- **The COP31 Presidency and the ITU** officially launched the Antalya AI Pledge, with an initial coalition of governments, companies, research institutions and civil society ✅ [1].
- **The Pledge** has four pillars: sustainable infrastructure and hardware; responsible lifecycle management; assessment and reporting of environmental impacts; support for developing countries ✅ [1].
- **The ITU** will track progress every year at the COP until 2030; its guidelines on AI's environmental impact will support implementation ✅ [1].
- **Tomas Lamanauskas, ITU Deputy Secretary-General**, said data-centre energy use for AI will double by 2030 ✅ [3].
- **Somalia, Uganda and Sierra Leone** endorsed it on Monday ⚠️ according to Climate Home [4]. **Bashir Jama, Somalia's environment minister**, called for accessible finance and affordable computing ⚠️ [4].
- **Simon Stiell, UNFCCC Executive Secretary**, said AI's benefits "must outweigh its growing costs" ⚠️ according to Climate Home [4].

## Announcements and numbers
- 80 endorsements and 271 commitments as of Monday morning, according to UN News ✅ [3]. Climate Home instead counts nine countries and over 40 companies ⚠️ [4].
- By 2030 AI data centres could use nearly 945 TWh of electricity a year ✅ [5].

## Quotes
- "Industry and technology must be part of the climate solution." — **Murat Kurum, COP31 President** [1]
- "AI is a powerful tool for tackling the climate crisis, but it must be used responsibly" — **Doreen Bogdan-Martin, ITU Secretary-General** [1]

## Relevance for the VSME
**High** — If cloud and AI providers report energy and emissions, SMEs will be able to request these data for their own Scope 3 (indirect value-chain emissions) and for the VSME energy and greenhouse gas disclosure (B3); those running their own servers should already measure their consumption.
