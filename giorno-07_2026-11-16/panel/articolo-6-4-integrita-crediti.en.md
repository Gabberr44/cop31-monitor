# UN carbon crediting market: push to loosen the rules
**When:** 16/11, time n/a · **Where:** CMA (the Conference of the Parties to the Paris Agreement) informal consultations, Antalya EXPO Center · **Organiser:** UNFCCC<br>
**Speakers:** **Malin Ahlberg** (Germany) and **Sonam Tashi** (Bhutan), co-facilitators on Article 6.4<br>
🎥 No video expected (informal consultations)

## At a glance
The PACM (the Paris Agreement Crediting Mechanism, Art. 6.4) applies conservative rules: when a reduction is uncertain, it issues fewer credits. Several countries, in line with industry associations, want these rules revisited; the EU, Switzerland and Senegal defend integrity.

## Key points
- **The Like-Minded Developing Countries (LMDCs, including Saudi Arabia, India and China), the Least Developed Countries and landlocked developing countries** complain that the cumulative weight of requirements blocks projects 🟡 according to Climate Home [1].
- **The draft decision** asks the Supervisory Body to avoid "excessive compounding of conservative adjustments" 🟡 [1][2].
- **The European Union** objected in the room to a reference to "avoiding over conservativeness and prescriptiveness": methodologies are prescriptive by nature 🟡 [1][2].
- **Singapore and some Latin American states** want rules that keep projects financially viable; **South Korea** asks for proportionate requirements ⚠️ according to Climate Home [1].
- **Environmental NGOs** warned that the mechanism must not become another "overcrediting mechanism" ⚠️ according to ENB [2].
- **IETA (International Emissions Trading Association), the Project Developer Forum and the Wildlife Conservation Society** had asked before the COP for an assessment of the rules' "combined impact" ⚠️ according to Climate Home [1].

## Announcements and numbers
- **Singapore** signed with **Kenya** its 13th implementation agreement on carbon credits (Art. 6.2) ⚠️ [3].
- A 2024 study in *Nature Sustainability* estimated cookstove projects were over-credited by roughly tenfold ⚠️ according to Climate Home [1].

## Quotes
- "Having a Paris Agreement carbon crediting mechanism that cheats the atmosphere completely misses the point." — **Isa Mulder, Carbon Market Watch** [1]

## Relevance for the VSME
**Medium** — Weaker rules mean less reliable credits: an SME offsetting emissions must keep them separate from its total GHG (greenhouse gas) emissions in the VSME and state the credits' type and standard.
