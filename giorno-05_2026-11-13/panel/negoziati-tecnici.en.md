# Technical negotiations: Article 6.4, finance, adaptation, technology
**When:** 13/11, informal consultations during the day · **Where:** Antalya EXPO Center, negotiation rooms · **Organiser:** subsidiary bodies SBI and SBSTA (the Convention's technical bodies), COP and CMA (the meeting of the Parties to the Paris Agreement) 🟡 [1]<br>
**Speakers:** co-facilitators and negotiating groups; **Mkhuthazi Steleki** (Chair of the Article 6.4 Supervisory Body) 🟡 [1]<br>
🎥 No video foreseen (informal sessions)

## At a glance
The subsidiary bodies are wrapping up their first-week work: some rooms agreed conclusions, others forward items to the second week. It matters for business on two topics: the UN carbon crediting mechanism and the reference to artificial intelligence.

## Key points
- **Mkhuthazi Steleki, Chair of the Article 6.4 Supervisory Body**, said the mechanism has moved "from design to delivery" and has issued its first credits 🟡 [1].
- **Mkhuthazi Steleki** reported 4 new methodologies in 2026, 5 in total, including clean cooking and nitrous oxide abatement 🟡 [1]. Without new funding, the mechanism will have no resources after 2027 🟡 [1].
- **Mitigation work programme**: **the Arab Group, African Group and Malaysia** want it to continue until 2030; **the EU and Mongolia** until 2040 🟡 [1].
- **National adaptation plans (NAPs)**: **the UK and EU** ask to reference private finance and enabling conditions 🟡 [1]. **The LMDCs (like-minded developing countries, including China and India)** oppose welcoming the report on finance flows for NAPs 🟡 [1].
- **Long-term finance**: Parties remain divided on whether the USD 100 billion goal was met, given the different calculations by the **OECD** and **Oxfam** 🟡 [1].
- **Technology**: in the TEC-CTCN joint report (the UN climate technology bodies) there is no agreement on referencing artificial intelligence and digital technologies 🟡 [1].

## Announcements and numbers
- **Fund for Responding to Loss and Damage**: **the African Group, Arab Group, Least Developed Countries (LDCs), AOSIS (small island states), China, the Philippines and Brazil** want CMA guidance to the Fund; **the EU, Japan, Canada, New Zealand and Norway** call it "micromanaging" 🟡 [1].
- **In the corridors**: Thursday evening's global Microsoft outage also slowed down emails and draft texts on Friday 🟡 [1].

## Relevance for the VSME
**Medium** — Article 6.4 methodologies define which UN credits exist: an SME that buys credits reports them separately from B3 emissions, with type and standard.
